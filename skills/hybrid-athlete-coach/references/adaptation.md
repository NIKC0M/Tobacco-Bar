# Adaptation rules

How today's session changes, and what a hybrid week should look like.

## Order of decision

Guardrails outrank feelings. Work down this list — the first match that bites
sets the ceiling, and later rules can only lower it further.

1. **Acute:chronic above 1.5** → cap load at ~70% of plan, whatever the athlete
   says. Load spikes hurt people who feel fine; that's what makes them spikes.
2. **Form below -25 *and* readiness compromised** → rest. Not a shortened hard
   session — rest, or a walk.
3. **Readiness compromised, form manageable** → downgrade the session's intent
   one step.
4. **Readiness moderate** → roughly 80% of planned load, same intent.
5. **Readiness primed, form positive, ACWR under 1.3** → the only situation in
   which adding is reasonable, and then by ~5%.
6. **Otherwise** → as written.

If confidence is below 0.5, pull whatever adjustment you landed on toward
neutral — roughly `1 + (multiplier - 1) × confidence × 2` — and say why.

## Downgrade ladder

Changing what kind of session it is beats shortening it. Recovery cost tracks
intensity more than duration, so a 25-minute VO2max session is still a hard day.

| Planned | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| VO2max / sprint | Tempo |
| Threshold / hard conditioning | Easy aerobic |
| Tempo | Easy aerobic |
| Long endurance | Shorter easy aerobic |
| Max strength / power | Hypertrophy at RPE 7 |
| Hypertrophy | Movement quality, light |

## Session templates

Fill in the athlete's own heart-rate numbers rather than naming zones alone.

- **Recovery** — 20–40 min, 50–60% heart-rate reserve. Conversational. If in
  doubt, slower.
- **Easy aerobic** — 40–75 min, 60–70% HRR. Nose-breathing pace, no surges.
- **Long endurance** — 75–150 min mostly 60–70% HRR, optional lift to 70–80% in
  the last 10 minutes. Fuel from 45 minutes on.
- **Tempo** — 15 min warm-up, 20–40 min at 70–80% HRR, 10 min down.
- **Threshold** — 15 min warm-up, 3–4 × 8–12 min at 80–90% HRR with 2 min float,
  10 min down.
- **VO2max** — 15 min warm-up, 5–6 × 3 min at 90%+ HRR with 3 min easy, 10 down.
- **Max strength** — 5×3 at 82–87% of estimated 1RM, 3 min rest, stopping 1–2
  reps shy of failure.
- **Hypertrophy** — 3–4 compounds, 3×8–10 at RPE 7–8, 90 s rest, 2 reps in
  reserve.
- **Power** — 6×2 at 70–75%, maximum bar speed, set ends when speed drops.

## Concurrent training

The interference effect is real but frequently overstated. What reliably matters:

- **Six hours** between hard endurance and heavy lower-body lifting. Under that,
  both adaptations are blunted. If the day can't accommodate it, name the
  priority session and let the other one be the compromise.
- **Order within a day**: whichever quality is the priority goes first, on fresh
  legs. Endurance-before-lifting costs strength; lifting-before-endurance costs
  the aerobic session less.
- **Upper-body lifting doesn't interfere meaningfully** with running. It's the
  legs that collide.
- **Easy aerobic work doesn't interfere** with strength at all, and helps
  recovery. The conflict is specifically hard endurance vs heavy legs.

## Weekly shape

- Two hard days, ideally three, with easy or rest between. Hard days back to back
  are the most common self-inflicted injury.
- Roughly 80% of endurance time genuinely easy. Hybrid athletes drift their easy
  runs into moderate, which fatigues without adapting — "grey zone" training.
- Weekly load ramp under ~10% in a build phase. Under 5% when peaking.
- Every fourth week at ~60–65% load. If the athlete hasn't deloaded in four
  weeks and form keeps sliding, that's usually the answer before anything clever.
- Check the endurance/strength split against their stated emphasis. If they say
  balanced but the last three weeks ran 85/15, that's worth raising — it's the
  most common way a hybrid block quietly becomes a running block.

## Phases

| Phase | Focus | Ramp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | Aerobic volume, strength foundation | ≤8%/wk |
| Build | Threshold work, max strength | ≤10%/wk |
| Peak | Race-specific intensity, volume drops | ≤5%/wk |
| Taper | Intensity held, volume down 40–60% | negative |
| Deload | Everything down ~35% | negative |

Taper detail worth getting right: **keep the intensity, cut the volume.** Going
easy for a fortnight before an event loses sharpness. Short, fast, well-rested
work maintains it.
